Information desk

Questions a judge would ask.

Does the AI decide the verdict?

No. qwen4-max reads the notice and proposes fields, each with the quote it came from. Plain code checks every field against CMC rows, and a table of ten rules (R1–R10, successor/verify/verdict.py) picks the verdict.

Why can't one API call answer this?

CMC's info endpoint returns the notice as prose. No field says "the successor of CPOOL is CLEAR, 1:1". Something has to read it, and something else has to check what was read.

What is live, and what is cached?

Every lookup calls CMC live, 5 to 12 calls in the captured streams. The Reader's output is cached per coin and notice text, so a click costs at most one agent read (up to 3 Qwen calls) and none when the notice is cached. Cached p50 is 1.007 s; a cold read's p50 is 15.44 s (both measured 2026-11-22).

What if the notice is wrong?

Then a check fails and the verdict says so. In the census, 26 proposed successors fail a check against CMC's records, and a Reader quote that is not in the notice is dropped before any check runs.

Why do most coins end NOT VERIFIABLE?

Because CMC's data cannot confirm them. In 61 of the 130, old and new token share one CMC page, so there are no two price series to test a ratio against. Successor shows the reason instead of guessing.

Is this financial advice?

No. The verdict is what CMC's data supports. Values exclude fees, gas and slippage, and a SWAP reads "if the issuer's swap is still open" when the notice states no window.
